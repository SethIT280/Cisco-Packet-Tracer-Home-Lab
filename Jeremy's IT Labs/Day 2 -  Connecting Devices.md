# Day 2 - Connecting Devices

## Task
Connect each device using the appropriate type of cable.

<img src="../Jeremy's IT Labs/images/day2-img1.png" width="600" alt="Complete DHCP configuration wizard">


## What I Did
- Connected routers, switches, and endpoints using the correct cable type
  - PC to switch: straight-through cable
  - Switch to switch: crossover cable
  - Switch to router: straight-through cable
  - Router to router: crossover cable
  - Connected routers at 250 m using multimode fiber
  - Connected routers at 3 km using single-mode fiber

## What I Learned
- Ethernet standards are listed by speed, common name, IEEE standard, informal name, and max length
  - Example: 10 Gigabit Ethernet, 802.3an, 10GBASE-T, 100 m
- 10BASE-T and 100BASE-T use different transmit and receive pins on different devices
  - Example: a PC transmits on pins 1 & 2 and receives on pins 3 & 6, while a switch transmits on pins 3 & 6 and receives on pins 1 & 2
- Straight-through vs. crossover cable
  - Straight-through: each pin connects to the same pin on both ends
  - Crossover: the transmit pins on one end connect to the receive pins on the other
- Fiber-optic standards are listed by informal name, IEEE standard, speed, cable type, and max length
  - Example: 10GBASE-ER, 802.3ae, 10 Gbps, single-mode, 30 km
- Single-mode vs. multimode fiber
  - Single-mode: smaller core, longer range, more expensive
  - Multimode: wider core, shorter range, less expensive
